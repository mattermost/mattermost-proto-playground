Layout keeps the product feeling structured and predictable across platforms
and screen sizes. Grids, breakpoints, and persistent panels hold every surface
together — and interaction targets keep them usable.

## Grids

### 8px grid

Most layout decisions snap to an 8px grid. Component widths, heights, and the
gaps between them should land on whole multiples of 8 wherever possible.

<GridOverlay size={8} caption="8px grid — the default rhythm" />

### 4px grid

Type, icons, and other small elements that can't comfortably hit 8px land on a
4px grid instead. The 4px grid is a refinement, not a replacement — only step
down when 8px doesn't fit.

<GridOverlay size={4} caption="4px grid — for type, icons, and small elements" />

### Vertical baseline grid

A 4px baseline grid sets the vertical rhythm for type. It exists because the
base body size (14px) has an x-height of 8px — half of that gives us a 4px
baseline that's still flexible enough for line-height tweaks. Body line heights
should always be a multiple of 4px; reach for 8px when nothing smaller is
needed.

### Horizontal grid

A horizontal grid keeps pages feeling grounded and helps users scan content.
Aligning content blocks to shared columns is what turns a screen full of
elements into a layout.

## Grid anatomy

<GridAnatomy />

### Columns

Columns span the main content region. Content blocks place into one or more
columns. Column widths are expressed as percentages so layouts stay flexible
across screen sizes. The number of columns is set by the breakpoint.

### Gutters

Gutters are the fixed-width gaps between columns that separate adjacent
content. Gutter width is fixed within a breakpoint but can grow at larger
breakpoints to give the layout more breathing room.

### Margins

Margins are the fixed-width space between the grid and the edges of the
viewport. Like gutters, margins are fixed within a breakpoint and can grow at
larger ones. **Margins should always be greater than or equal to the gutter.**

### Layout regions

A layout region is any area that holds content and aligns to the grid. Regions
can span the full set of columns or only a subset — an _offset layout_ that
occupies fewer columns and centers within the page.

## Fixed-width panels

Persistent panels — the channel sidebar on the left, the right-hand sidebar
when expanded — sit outside the responsive grid. The grid that drives the
center channel is offset by each visible panel's width and recalculates its
columns to fit the remaining space.

<SidebarOffset />

When both sidebars are visible the available width contracts further, and
larger breakpoints fall back to the column count of the next smaller
breakpoint.

## Interaction targets

An interaction target is the area of an element that responds to a click or
tap. Adequately sized targets are critical for usability — especially on
touch.

<InteractionTargets />

### Minimum click area

For pointer devices, the minimum hit area is **24×24px** with at least
**8px** between adjacent targets.

### Minimum tap area

For touch devices, the minimum tap area is **40×40px** with at least **8px**
between targets. In tight regions like dense toolbars, spacing can drop to
**4px** — but never below.
