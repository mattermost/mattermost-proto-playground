Global Membership Policies · who belongs in a channel

Two abilities we need to control channel membership at scale

A membership policy decides who belongs in a channel. To make that work across thousands of channels, we need two abilities. This deck shows what each one does, where we'd use it, and what we can't do without it.

The two abilities, in plain terms
1

Match a person to the channel

Compare a person's own information against the channel's own information — not a fixed value typed in once.

For example: let people in only if their clearance is high enough for that channel.
2

Point a policy at channels by their labels

Apply one policy to every channel that carries a certain label, instead of picking channels one by one.

For example: apply the Dragon Spacecraft rules to every channel labeled Dragon Spacecraft.
Ability 1 · what it does

Match a person to the channel

The person
Clearance: Secret
must be ≥
This channel
Classification: Secret

A rule compares the person's information to the channel's own information. Write it once, and it works for every channel — because each channel brings its own values to the comparison. No need to write a separate rule for each level.

Ability 1 · where we'd use it

Match a person to the channel

1Clearance vs. classification. Join only if your clearance meets that channel's classification.
2Program access. Join only if you're read into that channel's program.
3Country vs. releasability. Join only if your country is allowed (US-only, Five Eyes, NATO).
4Special-access caveat. Join only if you hold the caveat the channel requires.
5Organization / unit. Join only if you're in the unit the channel belongs to.
6Mission / task force. Join only if you're assigned to that channel's mission.
7Location / theater. Join only if you're posted to that channel's region.
8Contract / vendor. Join only if you're on the contract the channel is tied to.
9Certification level. Join only if your certification meets the level the channel needs.
10Duty status. Join only if your status matches — for example, active duty only.
Ability 1 · what we can't do without it

Without this, we can't match people to channels

✕

We can't write one rule that fits every channel. Each rule would hold a single fixed value.

✕

To cover the whole workspace, we'd hand-build a separate policy for every value — each clearance level, each program, each country — and keep them all in sync.

✕

A new channel, or a channel that changes level, wouldn't be covered until someone builds or edits a matching policy by hand.

Outcome

People stay in channels they shouldn't be in, and the policy list grows too large to review or trust.

Ability 2 · what it does

Point a policy at channels by their labels

One policy
Dragon Spacecraft rules
applies to →
Every channel labeled
Program: Dragon Spacecraft

Instead of listing channels by hand, the policy points at a label. Every channel with that label is covered — and a new channel is covered the moment it gets the label. Nothing to remember, nothing to keep updating.

Ability 2 · where we'd use it

Point a policy at channels by their labels

1One program's channels. Apply a program's rules to every channel labeled with it.
2One classification level. Apply the Top Secret rules to every Top Secret channel.
3One mission / task force. The rules follow every channel that mission creates.
4One organization / command. Cover all of a command's channels at once.
5One contract / engagement. Cover every channel for a given contract.
6One region / theater. Cover every channel in a theater.
7One coalition / partner. Cover every channel shared with a partner nation.
8One sensitivity type. Cover every channel marked "restricted handling."
9Hand off control. Give a command charge of just its own channels.
10One exercise / event. Cover every channel created for a specific exercise.
Ability 2 · what we can't do without it

Without this, coverage is always a step behind

✕

We can only pick channels one by one, or apply to every channel.

✕

A new channel isn't covered until someone adds it by hand. Until then, it has no membership rules.

✕

Coverage falls out of date every time channels are created, renamed, retired, or re-labeled.

✕

We can't cleanly give one program or command control of just their own channels.

Outcome

Channels get missed, so the wrong people can join or stay — and admins keep maintaining lists that are never complete.

What we're asking for

Support both abilities in the membership policy editor

They're already designed and built in a working prototype. Together they let a security team set who belongs in a channel the way their organization already works — by clearance, program, country, and mission — and keep it correct as channels change.

This is the standard way access is defined in Zero Trust systems (NIST SP 800-162). Most tools we looked at can't compare a person to a channel this way, or point a policy at channels by their labels — so it's a real advantage for defense and regulated customers.
